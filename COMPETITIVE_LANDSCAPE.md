# Competitive Landscape

This document captures the competitive conclusions from product discovery. It must be revalidated with current market research before launch.

## Adjacent competitors

### General local discovery

- Google Maps
- Yelp
- Apple Maps

Strengths:

- Huge store databases
- Ratings
- Reviews
- Hours
- Photos
- Navigation
- Existing user adoption

Weakness for this use case:

- Generic business discovery
- Limited antique-specific attributes
- Weak preference matching for antique categories
- Weak active management of a changing shopping-day itinerary

### Antique directories

Examples identified during research included AntiqueTrail.com and smaller regional directories.

Strengths:

- Existing relationships with antique stores
- Directory focus
- Printed maps and brochures
- Proof that store-based physical promotion can work

Weakness:

- Often directory- and advertising-oriented
- Limited personalized taste profile
- Limited live hours-aware route execution
- Limited private find and collection workflow

### Road-trip planners

Examples include Roadtrippers and similar itinerary products.

Strengths:

- Multi-stop planning
- Route discovery
- Travel organization

Weakness:

- General-purpose
- Do not understand antique-store browsing duration
- Do not prioritize stores by collector preference
- Do not connect trips to private finds and collection history

### Antique identification apps

The market is crowded with photo identification and valuation apps.

Strengths:

- Clear phone-camera use case
- Strong consumer interest
- Easy marketing promise

Weakness:

- Item-focused rather than store- and trip-focused
- Accuracy and trust concerns
- Identification features are becoming commoditized

Product implication:

Do not position the first release as another AI antique identifier.

### Collection trackers

Strengths:

- Photos
- Custom fields
- Export
- Inventory

Weakness:

- Not connected to store discovery
- Not connected to route planning
- Not connected to store recommendations
- Not connected to partner voting and store visits

## Market gap

No dominant product was identified that clearly combines:

1. Public antique-store directory
2. Public ratings and reviews
3. Personal taste matching
4. Store-hours-aware route planning
5. Active trip management
6. Private find capture
7. Private collection history
8. Store-owner participation

## Positioning

Primary statement:

> Helping antique lovers discover their next favorite store.

Functional promise:

> Find antique stores you are likely to enjoy and visit more of them before they close.

## Strategic warning

The application competes indirectly with very large platforms.

It should not attempt to beat Google at general mapping, Yelp at general reviews, or AI-identification products at generic photo appraisal.

It should win by deeply serving the antique-shopping workflow.

## Primary business risks

- Store-directory cold start
- Store-hour accuracy
- Duplicate records
- Store closures
- Review abuse
- Mapping API costs
- User acquisition
- Store-owner trust
- Regional data density
- Legal disputes over reviews or photos

## Recommended launch wedge

Build the best antique-store directory and trip planner for one region.

Use:

- Verified listings
- Local store partnerships
- Printed flyers
- QR codes
- Claim-your-listing workflow
- Useful free store profiles
- Strong mobile trip planner
- Reliable support

Expand region by region after proving engagement and store participation.
