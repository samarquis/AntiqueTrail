## Emotional register indexed from approved authority

Antique Trail is quiet, trustworthy, legible, locally curious, and archival without nostalgic clutter. It should feel like a practical field guide assembled with care—not an antique-shop costume, luxury marketplace, or themed scrapbook.

**Daylight Archive** is the light expression: calm stone and ivory surfaces, clear blue-black reading, restrained slate-blue action, and attention colors used only for meaning. **Midnight Archive** is the same identity after dark: charcoal depth, soft ivory reading, dusty-blue action, aged-brass attention, and weathered-clay danger. Review both as one system, not competing brands. Exact palette decisions remain in [DESIGN_SYSTEM color](../../DESIGN_SYSTEM.md#color).

## Visual do and don't

| Do                                                                                    | Don't                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Use quiet hierarchy, generous reading space, and practical evidence.                  | Add decorative antique clutter or generic vintage collage treatments.                   |
| Let slate, stone, ivory, charcoal, brass, and clay communicate their governed roles.  | Revive teal, mint-glass, bottle-green, parchment, sepia, or Field-and-Brass styling.    |
| Use the approved display face only for concise hierarchy and the UI face for reading. | Use distressed type, faux letterpress, ornamental scripts, or barnwood textures.        |
| Show age and locality through real store details and useful photography.              | Manufacture nostalgia with stamps, torn paper, dust, filigree, or antique-shop costume. |
| Preserve the same calm structure in light, dark, and forced-colors modes.             | Treat dark mode as a dramatic rebrand or forced colors as an optional polish pass.      |

## Imagery

- Prefer practical storefront, entrance, aisle, hours, accessibility, and merchandise-detail images that help a shopper decide or navigate.
- Label fictional or synthetic review imagery where the controlling product contract requires disclosure. Never let generated review fixtures imply a real store, owner relationship, paid placement, or endorsement.
- Eventual real-store photography requires approved provenance, rights, accurate alternative text, and the exact consent boundary in product and trust authority.
- Do not use generic vintage collages, scraped social imagery, automatic website screenshots, unrelated stock nostalgia, or provider content as catalog decoration.
- When imagery is unavailable, preserve the listing hierarchy with a calm, labeled placeholder. Do not hide the store, invent a photo, or let the empty image treatment dominate the card.

## Mood critique decision rule

A Mood review passes only when the reviewer cites a concrete rule above, checks every audience row in light and dark themes, includes forced-colors behavior where visual styling is suppressed, and finds no contradiction with the linked controlling authority. Adjectives without route evidence do not pass.
